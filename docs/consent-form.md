## What this study is

A computer program watches a webcam picture of a person at a desk. It looks for signs of tiredness (closing the eyes, yawning, nodding), and it checks that the person at the desk is who they say they are. To find out how well it works, the researcher needs recordings and test attempts from real people.

## What you will be asked to do (pick only what you agree to)

| Part | What happens | Time |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Fatigue recording** | You sit at a computer. The screen tells you when to close your eyes, yawn, nod your head, talk, or look down. This is **acted**, not real tiredness. A video of your face (no sound) is recorded. | about 6 minutes, once or twice |
| **B. Face matching** | The program turns your face into a list of numbers (a "template"). You then look at the camera a few times, in different light and with or without glasses. **Photos are not kept**, only the numbers. | about 10 minutes |
| **C. Fake-face tests** (only for the person being copied) | The researcher shows the program a printed photo, a phone photo or a video of **you** to see whether it can be fooled. This needs a photo or video of you, which is kept only as long as the tests need it. | about 10 minutes |
| **D. Practice calls** (optional) | You talk by voice with an AI caller. Your speech is sent to a speech-to-text service (Groq) to turn it into words; the words are stored with the call. | about 15 minutes |

## What is kept, where, and for how long

- **Videos (part A) and photos or videos for part C:** stored **only on the researcher's computer**, in a private folder outside any cloud-synced folder. They are used only to test the program.
- **Face numbers (part B):** stored on the researcher's computer. They cannot be turned back into a picture of you.
- **Results:** tables and charts that contain **no faces**. They show only counts and rates, for example "8 of 10 yawns were found".
- **Part D:** the call transcripts and timings are stored in a research database (Supabase). The voice audio is sent to Groq to be transcribed and is **not stored by the researcher**; Groq's own retention terms apply (`[check and write them here]`).
- **Retention:** everything is deleted `[for example: when the thesis is accepted, or at most 12 months after you take part]`.
- **Pictures in the thesis or a presentation:** none of your pictures or video will appear without a **separate written permission** from you.

## Your rights

- You decide **which parts** you take part in, and you can say no to any of them without giving a reason.
- You can **stop at any time** (the recording tool stops with the Esc key).
- You can **ask to see or delete** everything kept about you, at any time, by contacting the researcher. Deleting means the recordings, the face numbers and your test attempts.
- Taking part or not has **no effect** on your grades, work or anything else.
- You may complain to the National Privacy Commission (privacy.gov.ph) if you believe your data was mishandled.

## Risks and benefits

There is no medical or physical risk. The main risk is privacy, which is why data stays on one computer and results carry no faces. There is no payment `[change if there is]`. The benefit is helping to test whether a webcam can fairly and safely help detect drowsiness.
